How it works

Torque turns into clamp load through friction: T = K x d x F. Most of your effort fights friction in the threads and under the head, not stretching the bolt. That is why oiled threads need less torque for the same clamp load, and why the same wrench setting can overshoot when threads are slippery.

What you get

  • Torque in N·m and ft·lbf for M5 to M39, classes 4.6, 8.8, 10.9 and 12.9
  • Clamp load, proof load and breaking load from ISO 898-1 strengths
  • Dry, oiled or zinc-flake threads, adjustable preload
  • A reverse check: what a torque you applied really gives, with a friction scatter range
  • A full size chart for the class you chose; runs fully in your browser
Advisory only. K values are estimates, torque is an indirect measure of tension. For wheels, brakes, pressure parts and structure use the manufacturer's spec. Source: Fastenal torque-tension table, ISO 898-1
